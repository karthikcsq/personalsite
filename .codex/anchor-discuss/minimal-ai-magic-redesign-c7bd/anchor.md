## Domain
- Product / creative direction

## Starting intent
- Redesign the personal website on a new branch.
- Replace the current maximalist presentation with a visually minimal surface.
- Make the apparent simplicity conceal unusually responsive, dynamic, AI-native behavior.
- Preserve a feeling of speed while making the experience feel new and magical.
- Build something behaviorally similar in spirit to Nemaas: a simple portfolio surface with a deeply crafted interactive world, while integrating AI and genuine nondeterminism into that world.

## Goals
- Brainstorm multiple highly creative interaction concepts before settling on a direction.
- Find a concept whose magic is behavioral, not merely decorative.
- Use generated images if they materially help demonstrate an experience.
- Make AI-literate visitors react: "holy shit, this guy is smart, and his website is so cool."
- Let visitors discover depth through the corpus, notes, and blog writing rather than reducing Karthik to bullet points.

## Non-goals
- No explicit non-goals established yet.
- Do not prematurely commit to implementation details during the initial creative discussion.

## Constraints and taste
- Clean and minimal in appearance.
- AI should create meaningful behavior, not a conventional chatbot pasted onto a portfolio.
- Dynamic, responsive, novel, and fast.
- High appetite for creative exploration.
- The interface may reshape itself radically in response to the visitor.
- The corpus is the enduring identity layer; the visible interface is allowed to be ephemeral.
- Some experience-level nondeterminism is desired: sessions and interactions should not always unfold identically.
- The visual world should still feel authored and coherent rather than like arbitrary model-generated UI.
- The initial state must make the site's four important content categories glanceable without requiring an AI interaction first: Work, Projects, Ideas, and Writing.
- The interactive world should have one dominant visual idea, not several competing mechanisms.
- Prefer recognizable, physical elements and environments over abstract generative dots, meshes, nodes, or data-art forms.
- Real natural elements such as sun, snow, trees, weather, birds, and tactile objects are part of what makes the Nemaas reference appealing.
- The generated `agents × verbatim` frame is a density benchmark only: extreme negative space, a compact information cluster, and one semantic event. Its dark palette, composition, linework, and specific visual language are not accepted design decisions.
- Real-world elements must preserve that benchmark's restraint and scale; they are isolated accents, not a fully illustrated environment.
- Solar Shadow is the leading daytime visual direction: a true-white field, small vermilion sun, and one sparse living shadow.
- Winter Branch is explicitly preferred as the corresponding night-mode look: a near-black field, one snow-dusted branch, and only a few flakes.
- Day and night should feel like two conditions of one site, not two separate visual systems or a reason to double the interface density.
- The notes themselves should be a first-class, persistent visual surface. Do not replace them with an AI-composed answer view that resembles chat without bubbles.
- Prefer AI that curates, reorders, connects, or reveals Karthik's exact prose over an AI narrator that summarizes the corpus back to the visitor.
- “Repainting” means the interface dynamically redraws the same corpus into a different minimal projection. It may change the background, spatial arrangement, visibility, labels, and environmental motion, but it must not accumulate more interface or prose.
- Dragging and physically combining notes should be a central interaction, not merely clicking filters in a conventional index.
- Use the large empty field as an active spatial surface. Motion, light, shadow, weather, and placement should carry meaning so dynamism does not require filling the page with words.
- The first view should show only Karthik's identity and four primary clickable portfolio domains—Work, Projects, Ideas, and Writing—then repaint the interface when a domain is opened. “Primary” describes their hierarchy, not oversized typography.
- The entire interface should occupy a compact portion of the viewport. Extreme negative space is preferable to billboard-scale category names or work entries.
- The daytime Solar Shadow composition should retain the stronger accepted natural elements from the earlier render: a clearly sized vermilion sun in the upper-left and a broad, soft leafy branch shadow entering from the lower-left. Do not shrink the sun into a status dot or replace the daytime leafy shadow with a bare winter twig.
- Winter Branch remains the separate night condition: bare/snow-dusted branch against near-black. Do not accidentally import its bare-branch grammar into the white daytime state.
- “Everything at a glance” means all four top-level domains, not all individual entries. Names, descriptions, notes, and semantic relationships belong one level deeper.
- Opening a domain must reveal its real entries with meaningful descriptions; names alone are not enough.
- Semantic exploration, AI labels/filters, and dragging or combining items begin inside an opened domain rather than competing for attention on the homepage.
- Semantic filtering must be automatic. Visitors should explore artifacts directly; they should not have to click a visible filter or lens control to make the map relevant.
- The interaction must reward curiosity with genuinely interesting, specific note headings and exact writing, not generic portfolio descriptions.
- Large physical metaphors such as paper strips, frosted windows, herbarium sheets, threads, and books are rejected as too visually heavy and insufficiently minimalist.

## Good drift criteria
- A change in visual or interaction direction is legitimate when Karthik explicitly prefers it after seeing the tradeoffs.
- Extra technical or content scope should earn its place by strengthening the minimal-surface / magical-behavior contrast.

## Agreement-drift risks
- Mistaking "AI" for a prominent chat box and losing the desired subtlety.
- Adding visual spectacle until the redesign becomes maximalist again.
- Treating novelty as more important than speed or clarity.
- Letting implementation convenience choose the concept before the experience is decided.

## Ambiguities (resolved)
- Q: Who should the site captivate first?
  A: People who know AI.
- Q: What should visitors understand or feel within 20 seconds?
  A: "Holy shit. This guy is smart, and his website is so cool."
- Q: What parts of the current site must remain unmistakably Karthik?
  A: The corpus: extensive notes, blogs, and other knowledge that let visitors learn more than bullet points.
- Q: How far may the interface personalize or reshape itself?
  A: Radically.
- Q: Is Nemaas merely a loose mood reference, or is a similar crafted interactive-world structure desired?
  A: A similar structure is the goal, with AI integrated somewhere inside it.
- Q: Should the experience be fully deterministic?
  A: No. Some amount of nondeterminism is explicitly desired.
- Q: Should the initial page hide the portfolio behind an interaction?
  A: No. The important content should be glanceable at the start.
- Q: Should the surrounding world be abstract or recognizable?
  A: Recognizable and physical. Real elements such as the sun, snow, and trees are explicitly desired.
- Q: Which generated image has the desired minimalist quality?
  A: The `agents × verbatim` semantic-connection frame, specifically for its amount of minimalism—not as the chosen design.
- Q: Which current visual concepts should lead the next iteration?
  A: Solar Shadow for day, paired with Winter Branch for night mode. The pairing is chosen; the exact semantic interaction remains open for iteration.
- Q: Should a semantic connection resolve into an AI-written answer with supporting quotes?
  A: No. That still feels like chat. The interface should display and organize the notes themselves more effectively.
- Q: What does “repainting” mean in this redesign?
  A: Dynamic minimalist recomposition: notes can move through the open canvas, be dragged together, acquire AI labels or filters, and cause the background and surrounding corpus view to transform without adding conversational UI.
- Q: Should a single tactile physical metaphor dominate the whole interface?
  A: No. The five physical-system concepts were explicitly rejected as non-minimal. The leading new proposal is an at-a-glance, text-first portfolio map that progressively opens into a semantic mind map.
- Q: What exactly must be glanceable on the base homepage?
  A: Only the four large clickable categories—Work, Projects, Ideas, and Writing—plus Karthik's identity. Clicking a category repaints the page to show its real entries and descriptions.
- Q: Should those four primary categories be physically large?
  A: No. Oversized category and company-name typography is visually maximalist. The whole interface should remain a compact cluster within a largely empty field.
- Q: How subtle should the Solar Shadow branch be?
  A: Use the earlier accepted balance: a broad but soft leafy shadow entering from the lower-left, clearly recognizable while staying outside the main reading area. “Minimal” should reduce information density, not erase the environmental composition.
- Q: How large and where should the daytime sun be?
  A: Restore the earlier Solar Shadow sun: a clearly visible vermilion disc in the upper-left, not a tiny upper-right status dot.
- Q: Should visitors manually choose semantic filters or lenses?
  A: No. The active lens should be inferred from focus, hover, drag, and the artifact pair; filtering should happen automatically as part of the repaint.
